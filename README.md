# Who Arranges the Ride? How Five States Run Medicaid Medical Transportation

How Pennsylvania, New York, New Jersey, Massachusetts, and Connecticut arrange non-emergency medical transportation (NEMT) for Medicaid members: who runs it, who holds the contracts, and what is changing in 2025–26. Current as of October 21, 2026.

**Interactive version:** [jphoebus.github.io/medicaid-transportation](https://jphoebus.github.io/medicaid-transportation/)

## Headline findings

- **Delivery models:** four of the five states hand rides to a broker. New York, New Jersey, and Connecticut each use one broker statewide, and Massachusetts uses two regional transit authorities. Pennsylvania runs rides through its 67 counties, with a state broker only in Philadelphia, and a 2026 state study recommended keeping that model.
- **Contracts and procurement:** New York's broker contracts end in July 2027, and the base term in Connecticut's 2023 RFP ends that September. Procurement has been contested in three states: New York's 2022 award drew a protest; Pennsylvania cancelled two Philadelphia solicitations after protests, and a third is stayed; and in New Jersey, an August 2026 protest decision sent the rebid back for award to the incumbent.

## What stands out

- **Brokers are the norm; Pennsylvania is the exception.** Pennsylvania's legislature stopped a statewide broker in 2019, and the state's June 2026 study found a broker's risks outweighed its benefits and focused on improving the county system.
- **Managed care hands off the ride.** New York carved rides out of managed long-term care in 2024, New Jersey's rules carve them out of managed care, and Pennsylvania's counties serve plan members. Massachusetts' integrated plans for older adults and people with disabilities arrange their own members' rides.
- **Procurement is the pressure point.** New York's award was protested, Pennsylvania's Philadelphia solicitation has failed twice and is in litigation, and New Jersey's rebid turned on a protest over a missing subcontractor form.
- **Payment models differ.** Connecticut's RFP weighed a per-member-per-month model with a separate administrative budget against full capitation; Philadelphia's broker is paid a capitated per-member-per-month rate; Massachusetts pays its brokers at cost, plus a management fee and a shared-ride incentive.
- **Legislatures are writing accountability rules.** New Jersey's A985, Connecticut's HB 5356, and New York's S4862 and A7329; Massachusetts' auditor found overpayments.

## What to watch

- Pending: New Jersey's final award on remand under bid 24DPP01040, which had not been posted as of Oct. 20, 2026. The current extension runs through Feb. 27, 2027.
- Pending: the litigation over Pennsylvania's Philadelphia solicitation, and DHS follow-through on updated MATP standards.
- 2027 sessions: New Jersey's A985, New York's S4862 and A7329, and Connecticut's rate-review proposal.
- July 31, 2027: New York's two broker contracts end.
- Sept. 30, 2027: the end of the base term set in Connecticut's 2023 RFP.

The question I expect to shape the next cycle: whether states keep buying medical transportation as a standalone contract, or start tying ride performance to the managed care and long-term care decisions that depend on it.

## About the data

The comparison is in [medicaid-transportation.csv](medicaid-transportation.csv), with one row per state and columns for delivery model, managed care members, broker or vendor, contract term, procurement status, legislation and studies, oversight and performance, oversight agency, sources, and last updated. Facts are drawn from state Medicaid agency pages, procurement notices, contract records, statutes and regulations, legislative records, and audits; where a detail rests on a secondary source, the file says "as reported." This comparison describes how states deliver rides, not which model or vendor is better, and is not legal or regulatory advice.
